\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Governed deployment of agentic artificial intelligence in regulated and
defense settings requires more than benchmark accuracy: it requires
\emph{checkable} guarantees about what a system did, why it was permitted to do
it, and whether the record can be tampered with after the fact. This is
\textbf{v24} of the Ouroboros thesis program of SZL Holdings, a DOI-pinned
lineage (v1--v23) whose subject is a machine-verified trust substrate resting on
three pillars: (i) the \emph{Ouroboros loop}, a bounded, well-founded
self-governing computation in which ``the loop is the product''; (ii) the
\emph{Lutar invariant} $\La_k(x) = \big(\prod_{i=1}^k x_i\big)^{1/k}$, an
equal-weight geometric-mean trust aggregator; and (iii) a \emph{proof-trail /
receipt architecture} formalized in Lean~4. The headline advance of v24 is an
\textbf{axiom-free conditional $\La$-uniqueness theorem}. In v23 the conditional
uniqueness of $\La$ was gated on a \emph{declared} project axiom (A6$'$
block-consistency). Here we report \lean{Lutar.Round13.lambda\_unique\_of\_separable}:
under $\{$A1, A2, A3, A5$\}$ together with \emph{slice-multiplicativity}
(separability), $\La$ is the unique aggregator, with
\printax{} $=$ \corecpc --- i.e.\ \textbf{no project axiom}, kernel-clean,
CI-green, merged to \lean{main} at \lean{b910c276}. This strictly strengthens
v23: the extra hypothesis is now a \emph{checkable property of} $\Phi$ (weaker
than the old \emph{Factors} premise) discharged through Mathlib and already-proved
in-tree lemmas, rather than a declared idealization. Crucially, the
\emph{unconditional} uniqueness of $\La$ under $\{$A1--A5$\}$ remains
machine-checked \textbf{false} (in-tree \lean{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda}; \lean{maxAgg}
and \lean{min} are A1--A5 counterexamples), so $\La$ stays \textbf{Conjecture~1}
unconditionally --- never a theorem. We also report a separate
\emph{experimental, CI-green} tier of frontier theorems (CF-13, CF-17, Wave-13
replay/quorum/HM results, and the Wave-14 pack CF-18/19/20/21), all
\printax{}-clean, that is never folded into the locked-five. The honesty
doctrine is binding throughout: locked-proven $=$ exactly five
$\{$F1, F11, F12, F18, F19$\}$; declared axioms disclosed; supply-chain posture
SLSA~L1 honest with L2 a roadmap (correcting a v23 overstatement); nothing
fabricated. We argue that replacing a declared axiom with a checkable property is
an \emph{epistemic upgrade}, and that this calibrated honesty is what makes every
positive claim credible to an auditor.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Agentic AI systems --- models that plan, call tools, retrieve, and act over many
steps --- are entering domains where the cost of an undetected error is not a bad
recommendation but a regulatory breach, a safety incident, or a compromised
mission. In such regulated and defense settings the operative question is no
longer ``how accurate is the model on a benchmark?'' but ``can the operator
\emph{prove}, after the fact and to a skeptical third party, that an action was
authorized, that the authorization rested on checkable evidence, and that the
record has not been altered?'' This is the problem of \emph{governed AI}: an
execution substrate in which trust is \emph{earned by checkable evidence} rather
than asserted by a vendor. The verifiable-claims program argues that the
industry default --- assert safety, keep the evaluation private, log to a mutable
store --- is structurally inadequate \cite{brundage2020}, and policy bodies have
echoed the demand that developers be able to \emph{prove} properties of their
systems \cite{ntia}.

This paper is \textbf{v24} of the Ouroboros thesis program, a DOI-pinned lineage
of twenty-three prior versions (v1--v23) archived under a Zenodo concept record
\cite{zenodoconcept}. Each prior version advanced a piece of the substrate; v23
``Unified Substrate'' consolidated them under an \emph{honesty doctrine}
(\S\ref{sec:doctrine}) that distinguishes, at every point of assertion,
\emph{proven} (kernel-verified, Lean-core axioms only) from \emph{proven under a
declared axiom} from \emph{conjectured}.

\paragraph{The v24 advance, stated precisely.}
v23's strongest $\La$-uniqueness result was \emph{conditional} and \emph{gated on
a declared project axiom}: \lean{lambda\_unique\_under\_block}, which assumed a
named \lean{A6'\_block\_consistent} idealization. The headline of v24 is that this
gate can be removed. The in-tree theorem
\begin{center}
  \fbox{\leantext{Lutar.Round13.lambda\_unique\_of\_separable}}
\end{center}
proves $\La$-uniqueness \emph{conditional} on $\{$A1, A2, A3, A5$\}$ together with
\emph{slice-multiplicativity} (separability), and its trusted base is exactly the
Lean core: \printax{} $=$ \corecpc --- \textbf{no project axiom}. It is
kernel-clean, CI-green, and merged to \lean{main} at \lean{b910c276}. This
\emph{strictly strengthens} v23: the extra hypothesis is now a \emph{checkable
structural property of} the candidate aggregator $\Phi$ --- and one weaker than
the old \emph{Factors} premise --- discharged through Mathlib and already-proved
in-tree lemmas, rather than a disclosed-but-unprovable idealization. We
characterize this as an \emph{epistemic upgrade} (\S\ref{sec:phil}).

\paragraph{What does \emph{not} change.}
The single most important honest statement in this program is unchanged and we
repeat it without softening: the \emph{unconditional} uniqueness of $\La$ under
$\{$A1--A5$\}$ is machine-checked \textbf{false}. The in-tree witness
\lean{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda} exhibits concrete A1--A5 aggregators (\lean{maxAgg} and
\lean{min}) that differ from $\La$. We therefore label $\La$
\textbf{Conjecture~1} unconditionally --- never a theorem --- exactly as v23 did.
v24 does not, and never will, claim otherwise.

\paragraph{The frontier tier.}
Alongside the headline theorem, v24 reports a tranche of new
\emph{experimental, axiom-free, CI-green} frontier theorems (\S\ref{sec:frontier}):
CF-13 (a deep-equilibrium input-Lipschitz well-posedness margin

\cite{higham2002}), the Wave-13 results (replay-root completeness, a

Every one is \printax{}-clean. None is folded into the locked-five.

\section{The Ouroboros loop: bounded self-governance}
\label{sec:loop}

The first pillar is a self-referential governed computation --- the
\emph{Ouroboros loop} --- in which the system's output feeds its own governance
input under a bounded, well-founded recursion: perceive, propose, gate, act,
record, and feed the record back. ``The loop is the product'': the governed
recurrence, with its receipts, is the unit of value, not a single inference.

\begin{definition}[Bounded Ouroboros loop]
A loop is \emph{bounded} if it admits a well-founded measure --- a ranking
function into a well-ordered set that strictly decreases on each governed step,
with no infinite descending chain.
\end{definition}

Termination is thereby guaranteed; self-reference is rendered coherent by
\emph{Tarskian stratification} (a governed object-level computation is audited by
a strictly higher meta-level), avoiding the self-verification paradoxes of
G\"odel and Tarski. The substrate does \textbf{not} claim to prove its own
consistency; instead proofs are checked by the Lean kernel, the kernel's run by
continuous integration, and CI by human sign-off. The bottom turtle --- the Lean
kernel and its core axioms, the declared cryptographic idealizations, and human
sign-off --- is finite and disclosed (\S\ref{sec:tcb}).

\section{The Lutar invariant $\La$ and its uniqueness boundary}
\label{sec:lambda}

\subsection{Definition and the aggregation axioms}
\label{sec:axioms}
A governed action accumulates evidence along several axes (provenance,
policy-compliance, measurability-honesty, moral-grounding, $\dots$), each a
sub-score in $[0,1]$. The governance question is how to combine $k$ sub-scores
into one trust verdict.

\begin{definition}[Lutar invariant]
For $x = (x_1,\dots,x_k)\in[0,1]^k$, the \emph{Lutar invariant} is the
equal-weight geometric mean
\[
  \La_k(x) \;=\; \Big(\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{k} x_i\Big)^{1/k}.
\]
\end{definition}

The geometric mean is the natural aggregator for \emph{ratio-scale} evidence: it
commutes with a common rescaling of all axes, and a single zero axis drives the
verdict to zero --- a disqualifying failure cannot be averaged away. The
governance axioms on a candidate aggregator $\Phi:[0,1]^k\to[0,1]$ are:

\begin{axiom}[Monotonicity, A1]\label{ax:a1}
$\Phi$ is non-decreasing in each argument.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Positive homogeneity, A2]\label{ax:a2}
$\Phi(c\cdot x) = c\,\Phi(x)$ for $c>0$ (scale-covariance).
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Idempotence / normalization, A3]\label{ax:a3}
$\Phi(c,\dots,c) = c$.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Boundedness, A4]\label{ax:a4}
$\Phi(x)\le \max_i x_i$.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Permutation invariance, A5]\label{ax:a5}
$\Phi(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)}) = \Phi(x)$ for every permutation
$\sigma$.
\end{axiom}

The classical theory (Kolmogorov 1930 \cite{kolmogorov1930}, Acz\'el 1948
\cite{aczel1948}, Hardy--Littlewood--P\'olya 1934) confirms that A1--A4 (and even
A1--A5) \emph{alone} do not force the geometric mean: the missing ingredient is a
\emph{bisymmetry / associativity / replacement} family
\cite{quasiarith,aczel1966}.

\subsection{The unconditional refutation: FALSE, machine-checked}
\label{sec:false}
It is tempting to claim $\La_k$ is the \emph{unique} $\Phi$ satisfying A1--A5.
\textbf{This is false, and we prove it false.}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation of unconditional uniqueness]
\label{thm:false}
\statusFalse\;\statusCI\;
There exists $\Phi\neq\La_k$ satisfying A1--A5. In particular the
max-aggregator $\mathrm{maxAgg}(x)=\max_i x_i$ satisfies A1--A5 yet differs from
$\La_k$: at $(4,1)$ (on the ratio scale) one has $\mathrm{maxAgg}=4$ while
$\La_2(4,1)=2$. The minimum aggregator $\min_i x_i$ is a second witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean reference]
The in-tree witness is \lean{Round13.maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda}. The max function is
monotone (A1), positively homogeneous (A2), idempotent (A3), bounded by itself
hence $\le\max$ (A4), and symmetric (A5); evaluation at $(4,1)$ separates it from
the geometric mean by \lean{decide}. \printax{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda} reports
Lean-core axioms only. \lean{min} is an analogous \lean{decide}-checked
companion.
\end{proof}

This is the epistemic heart of the program: we did not merely \emph{fail} to
prove unconditional uniqueness; we \emph{proved its negation}. Consequently $\La$
remains Conjecture~1 unconditionally (\S\ref{sec:conj}).

\subsection{The old route: uniqueness given factorization, and the A6$'$ gate}
\label{sec:oldroute}
The maximal honestly-true uniqueness statement of the prior lineage was
conditional on \emph{factorization}, fully proved in-tree.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness given factorization]
\label{thm:factors}
\statusSorryFree\;
Let $\Phi$ satisfy A1--A5 and suppose $\Phi$ \emph{factors}: there exist exponents
$\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k\ge 0$ with $\Phi(x)=\prod_i x_i^{\alpha_i}$ for all $x$.
Then $\Phi=\La_k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean reference and sketch]
The Lean term is \lean{lambda\_unique\_of\_factors} (Round-13). Given
factorization, idempotence (A3) forces $\sum_i\alpha_i=1$ and symmetry (A5)
forces all $\alpha_i$ equal, whence $\alpha_i=1/k$ and $\Phi=\La_k$. The exponent
collapse uses \lean{NNReal.rpow} arithmetic and a \lean{Finset} induction; the
companion \lean{lambda\_factors} (axiom-free, CI-green) shows $\La_k$ itself
factors with exponents $1/k$, so the hypothesis is non-vacuous.
\end{proof}

In v23 the substrate's \emph{recommended} route turned the opaque factorization
hypothesis into a governance-legible \emph{declared axiom}.

\begin{axiom}[Block-consistency / aggregation-invariance, A6$'$]\label{ax:a6p}
Aggregating evidence within independent blocks and then across the block results
equals aggregating the flattened collection; equivalently, the verdict is
invariant to how the auditor partitions evidence into review blocks (after
Cs\'at\'o 2018 \cite{csato2018}).
\end{axiom}

\begin{theorem}[v23 conditional uniqueness under declared A6$'$]
\label{thm:oldcond}
\statusAxiom\;\statusCI\;
Under $\{$A1--A5$\}$ together with the single declared axiom
\lean{A6'\_block\_consistent}, $\La_k$ is the unique normalized aggregator. Its
disclosed base is
\begin{center}
  \printax{lambda\_unique\_under\_block} $=$
  \leantext{[A6'\_block\_consistent,~propext,~Quot.sound,~Classical.choice]}
\end{center}
--- \emph{one declared, non-core project axiom} plus the Lean core.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:oldcond} was honest but carried a cost: the trusted base
included a \emph{project axiom} that could not itself be discharged in-kernel. v24
removes that cost.

\subsection{The v24 advance: axiom-free conditional uniqueness (CUT-2)}
\label{sec:cut2}
The v24 headline replaces the declared A6$'$ idealization with a \emph{checkable
structural property} of the candidate aggregator: \emph{slice-multiplicativity}
(separability). Informally, $\Phi$ is slice-multiplicative if it factors as a
product of per-axis slice functions, each of which is multiplicative, normalized,
and monotone --- properties one can in principle \emph{verify} of a concrete
$\Phi$, rather than postulate.

\begin{definition}[Slice-multiplicativity / separability]\label{def:sep}
$\Phi:[0,1]^k\to[0,1]$ is \emph{slice-multiplicative} if there exist slice
functions $f_i:\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ such that
\textnormal{(sep)}\, $\Phi(x)=\prod_{i} f_i(x_i)$,
\textnormal{(mul)}\, $f_i(s\,t)=f_i(s)\,f_i(t)$,
\textnormal{(one)}\, $f_i(1)=1$, and
\textnormal{(mono)}\, each $f_i$ is monotone.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Axiom-free conditional uniqueness of $\La$ --- the v24 advance]
\label{thm:cut2}
\statusSorryFree\;
Let $k>0$ and let $\Phi$ satisfy the Lutar axioms $\{$A1, A2, A3, A5$\}$. If
$\Phi$ is slice-multiplicative (Definition~\ref{def:sep}) then $\Phi=\La_k$. The
Lean term is \lean{lambda\_unique\_of\_separable} (namespace
\lean{Lutar.Round13}); its trusted base is exactly
\begin{center}
  \printax{lambda\_unique\_of\_separable} $=$ \corecpc,
\end{center}
i.e.\ \textbf{no project axiom}. It is kernel-clean, CI-green, and merged to
\lean{main} at \lean{b910c276}.
\end{theorem}

The exact Lean signature is:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
theorem lambda\_unique\_of\_separable \{k : $\mathbb{N}$\} (hk : 0 < k)\\
\hphantom{xx}($\Phi$ : Aggregator k) (hL : LutarAxioms $\Phi$)\\
\hphantom{xx}(f : Fin k $\to$ (NNReal $\to$ NNReal))\\
\hphantom{xx}(hsep\ : $\forall$ x, $\Phi$ x = $\prod$ i, f i (x i))\\

\hphantom{xx}(hone\ : $\forall$ i, f i 1 = 1)\\
\hphantom{xx}(hmono : $\forall$ i, Monotone (f i)) :\\
\hphantom{xx}$\Phi$ = $\Lambda$ k
\end{quote}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch and the in-tree lemma chain]
The proof reduces slice-multiplicativity to factorization and then discharges via
Theorem~\ref{thm:factors}. Concretely:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Per-axis power law.} For each axis $i$, the slice $f_i$ is
    multiplicative \textnormal{(mul)}, monotone \textnormal{(mono)}, and
    normalized \textnormal{(one)}. The in-tree lemma
    \lean{multiplicative\_monotone\_isPow\_pos} yields an exponent
    $\alpha_i$ with $f_i(t)=t^{\alpha_i}$ for all $t\neq 0$:
    \begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
    multiplicative\_monotone\_isPow\_pos \{f\} (hf\_mul) (hf\_mono) (hf\_one)\\
    \hphantom{xx}: $\exists\,\alpha$, $\forall$ t $\neq$ 0, f t = t\^{}($\alpha$ : $\mathbb{R}$)
    \end{quote}
    Boundary value $f_i(0)=0^{\alpha_i}$ follows from the idempotent dichotomy
    (\lean{slice\_zero\_idem}, \lean{slice\_const\_one\_of\_zero\_one}).
  \item \textbf{Exponent equality across axes.} The chosen exponents
    $\alpha_i$ are forced equal by permutation invariance (A5): applying
    \lean{Equiv.swap i j} to a two-hot test vector and using the in-tree
    injectivity lemma \lean{rpow\_left\_inj\_one\_lt} ($c^a=c^b\Rightarrow a=b$
    for $c>1$) gives $\alpha_i=\alpha_j$.
  \item \textbf{Assemble factorization.} Collecting the per-axis power laws yields
    the \lean{Factors} predicate ($\Phi(x)=\prod_i x_i^{\alpha_i}$).
  \item \textbf{Discharge.} Apply \lean{lambda\_unique\_of\_factors}
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:factors}); idempotence (A3) and symmetry (A5) collapse the
    exponents to $1/k$ and conclude $\Phi=\La_k$.
\end{enumerate}
The whole chain
\lean{multiplicative\_monotone\_isPow\_pos} $\to$ \lean{lambda\_unique\_of\_factors}
discharges only through Mathlib and already-verified, sorry-free Round-13 lemmas;
no new axiom token is introduced. Hence the trusted base is exactly the Lean core
\corecpc.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is strictly stronger than v23]
Slice-multiplicativity is logically \emph{weaker} than the \emph{Factors} premise
of Theorem~\ref{thm:factors} (it does not pre-suppose the explicit exponent
vector; the exponents are \emph{derived} via the power-law lemma), and it is a
\emph{checkable property of} $\Phi$ rather than a declared axiom about
aggregation. Theorem~\ref{thm:cut2} therefore (a) widens the gap between premise
and conclusion and (b) removes the project axiom from the trusted base that
Theorem~\ref{thm:oldcond} required. This is the sense in which v24 \emph{strictly
strengthens} the v23 conditional uniqueness result.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Structural correctness (sorry-free)}

and the folded state equals the last entry of the explicit replay trace (induction

$\mathrm{root}$, so re-verification fails.

a proof of cryptographic hardness --- disclosed in the \printax{} ledger. The

gates deny-by-default, and supports byte-identical deterministic replay (F1).

\section{Philosophical foundations: slice-multiplicativity as an epistemic upgrade}
\label{sec:phil}

\subsection{From a declared axiom to a checkable property}
A trusted base that contains a project axiom asks the auditor to \emph{believe}
something the kernel cannot check. A6$'$ (block-consistency) was such an axiom:
governance-legible, published, and defensible, but ultimately \emph{postulated}.
Slice-multiplicativity is different in kind. It is a structural \emph{property of
the candidate aggregator} $\Phi$ --- of the form ``$\Phi$ factors into
multiplicative, normalized, monotone slices'' --- that one can in principle
\emph{verify} of a concrete $\Phi$ by inspecting its definition. Replacing a
postulate the auditor must trust with a property the auditor can check is, on any
reliabilist account of justification \cite{reliabilism}, an \emph{epistemic
upgrade}: the warrant for the conclusion no longer routes through an unverifiable
idealization.

\subsection{When is a conditional legitimate?}
Four conditions, all met by Theorem~\ref{thm:cut2}:
(i) \emph{independently-motivated antecedent} --- separability is the standard
hypothesis under which multiplicative aggregators are characterized
\cite{aczel1966};
(ii) \emph{non-vacuous instantiation} --- $\La$ itself is slice-multiplicative
(\S\ref{sec:examples}), so the conditional is not vacuously true;
(iii) \emph{boundary established} --- without the hypothesis, uniqueness fails
(Theorem~\ref{thm:false});
(iv) \emph{disclosed at point of use} --- the \printax{} ledger shows the bare
core. The residual gap --- \emph{is slice-multiplicativity the philosophically
correct demand on a governed aggregator?} --- is genuinely open, which is exactly
why $\La$ remains Conjecture~1.

\subsection{Slice-multiplicativity vs.\ block-consistency}
Both A6$'$ and slice-multiplicativity are sufficient to pin $\La$. The relevant
difference is epistemic, not merely logical. A6$'$ entered the trusted base as a
named axiom; slice-multiplicativity enters as a \emph{hypothesis discharged
through the kernel}. The former widens the trusted computing base; the latter does
not. We therefore regard the v24 route as dominating the v23 route along the one
axis that matters to an auditor: \emph{how much must be trusted that cannot be
checked}.

\subsection{Steelman objections (with honest rebuttals)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{``Slice-multiplicativity is the geometric mean in costume.''} ---
    It is strictly weaker than the explicit \emph{Factors} premise and does not
    name the exponents; the power-law lemma \emph{derives} them. The residue is a
    defensible normative choice, labeled as Conjecture~1.
  \item \emph{``Motte-and-bailey.''} --- The conditional theorem and the bald
    ``$\La$ uniqueness'' are labeled and separated in the artifacts themselves;
    (U) is stated false.
  \item \emph{``Idealized crypto axioms make guarantees illusory.''} --- Conceded
    and disclosed; receipt guarantees are conditional on the same assumptions TLS
    and Certificate Transparency already rely on \cite{rfc6962}.
  \item \emph{``Arrow: aggregation is impossible.''} --- Arrow concerns ordinal
    preference aggregation; $\La$ aggregates cardinal ratio-scale evidence about
    one object, outside Arrow's IIA impossibility.
  \item \emph{``G\"odel/Tarski: self-auditing is incoherent.''} --- Conceded as a
    limit; the substrate uses bounded recursion verified by an external stratified
    stack, not self-verification.
\end{enumerate}

    \lean{quorum\_agreement\_single\_valued\_vote} is an explicitly non-Byzantine
    shadow, not a resolution.
  \item \textbf{DPO \lean{klDivergence\_nonneg} / \lean{pinsker} are false as
    stated} (no simplex constraint on \lean{PolicyParam}); they remain honest
    declared axioms. CF-21 proves the correctly-stated log-sum/Gibbs core but does
    \emph{not} repair them.
  \item \textbf{Open sorries remain.} The locked kernel carries 163 sorries; the
    experimental scope carries 307 raw sorries. RS Singleton upper bound / full
    MDS equality, the in-tree VCG file, Bekenstein/Bousso IQ-gate stubs, and the
    hard sites (Putnam analysis, xoshiro period via $\mathrm{GF}(2)^{256}$
    primitivity, Hoeffding--Azuma, Brouwer/cohomology, gauge symmetry,
    path-integral convergence) stay honest sorries with references intact.
  \item \textbf{SLSA L1 honest, L2 roadmap.} Not L2-verified, L3, FedRAMP,
    Iron~Bank, or CMMC (\S\ref{sec:slsacorrection}).
  \item \textbf{Declared idealizations.} Tamper-evidence, attribution, and Merkle
    binding hold only under declared collision-resistance / unforgeability axioms,
    disclosed in every \printax{} ledger.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} The substrate verifies a governance aggregator and receipt
    layer, not an end-to-end AI system; it is far smaller in scope and maturity
    than a fully verified OS kernel.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

v24 advances the Ouroboros program by a single, sharply-stated step: the central
trust aggregator's conditional uniqueness, which in v23 rested on a declared
block-consistency axiom, now rests on \emph{no project axiom at all}. The in-tree
\lean{lambda\_unique\_of\_separable} proves $\La$-uniqueness under
$\{$A1,A2,A3,A5$\}$ plus slice-multiplicativity with
\printax{} $=$ \corecpc, kernel-clean and CI-green at \lean{b910c276}, and the extra
hypothesis is a checkable property of $\Phi$ rather than an idealization. The
unconditional claim remains machine-checked \emph{false}, so $\La$ stays
Conjecture~1, exactly as before.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The one-line thesis.} \emph{v24 strictly strengthens the v23 conditional
uniqueness of the Lutar invariant by eliminating the declared block-consistency
axiom: $\La$-uniqueness is now proven conditional on $\{$A1,A2,A3,A5$\}$ plus a
checkable slice-multiplicativity property with a trusted base of exactly the bare
Lean core, while its unconditional uniqueness remains machine-checked false ---
so the axiom-free conditional theorem is the maximal true statement and the
invariant is honestly labeled Conjecture~1, never a theorem.}
\end{quote}

The contribution is not a new theorem alone but a new \emph{standard}: axiom
elimination on the load-bearing result, locked counts held separate from an
experimental tier, disclosed axioms, and a machine-checked refutation guarding the
one tempting overclaim. We submit that this calibrated honesty is precisely what a
regulator, an auditor, or a defense customer should require.
