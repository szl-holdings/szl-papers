%% ---------- Abstract --------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Agentic and autonomous artificial intelligence is \emph{post-deterministic}:
the same prompt, model, and tools may produce different action sequences, so
accountability cannot rest on re-deriving a deterministic trace.  We present
\textbf{Governed Post-Determinism (GPD)}, a machine-checked, cryptographically-receipted
substrate that wraps a post-deterministic agent in checkable bounds, attested
records, and replicated state, so that every governed inference carries a
verifiable warrant.

GPD is grounded in eight \emph{locked-proven} Lean~4 formulas---
$\{$F1, F4, F7, F11, F12, F18, F19, F22$\}$---whose count is itself a
machine-certified theorem (\texttt{locked\_count\_eight}, by \texttt{decide}),
pinned at kernel commit \texttt{c7c0ba17} (749\,decl / 14\,axioms /
163\,\texttt{sorry} obligations).  Every governed inference emits a
Dead-Simple Signing Envelope (DSSE) / ECDSA-P256 receipt deposited into a
self-hosted Merkle transparency log; the receipt is offline-verifiable and
carries a Green Software Foundation SCI energy field.  The producing artifact
is attested to SLSA Build Level 1 and Level~2 (\emph{not} L3, which is a
roadmap item).

Three results sit at the precise boundary of what can and cannot be proven
unconditionally.  The \emph{Lutar Invariant}~$\Lam$ (equal-weight geometric
mean) is \textbf{Conjecture~1}: its unconditional uniqueness under the
aggregation axioms is machine-checked \emph{false}
(\texttt{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda}), while its conditional uniqueness under
slice-multiplicativity is \textbf{Theorem~U}---proven axiom-free with
$\texttt{\#print axioms}\subseteq\Ucoreaxioms$ (no project axiom).
Khipu BFT agreement is \textbf{Conjecture~2}: unconditional safety is open,
while conditional agreement under $n\ge 3f+1$ and honest non-equivocation is
proven axiom-clean.  Khipu liveness is \textbf{Conjecture~3}.

The E8 lattice is cited for error-\emph{detection} geometry
(citing Viazovska 2017), never as a security or BFT primitive.  The
Bekenstein bound appears as a physical analogy (F19), not a reclaimed result.
The counter-UAS application (\emph{killinchu}) demonstrates the substrate
in an auditable-interdiction setting.  No ATO, IL5, or FedRAMP authorization
is claimed; trust is never~$100\%$.

The paper's primary contribution is the \emph{machine-checked formalization +
governed-inference pipeline + verifiable-receipt substrate}, not new physics
or pure mathematics; all classical results are attributed to their original
authors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The governance gap in post-deterministic AI}

Classical software governance rests on a substrate of determinism: an auditor
who doubts a program's output can re-run it on the recorded inputs and obtain
the recorded outputs.  This assumption does not hold for modern agentic AI.  A
large-model agent that plans, calls external tools, and revises its plan is
\emph{post-deterministic}: its control flow is sampled, its environment is
non-stationary, and the ``same'' task may produce different---yet individually
defensible---action sequences.  Re-derivation no longer establishes
accountability.

The gap is real and commercially significant.  Defense and regulated-industry
customers increasingly require that AI decisions carry a \emph{warrant}---a
tamper-evident record of the evidence on which the decision was based and the
rules under which it was authorized---yet no industrially deployed substrate
provides one with a machine-checked foundation.  Governance platforms such as
Arize~\cite{arize2024}, Credo~AI~\cite{credoai2024}, and Fiddler~\cite{fiddler2024}
instrument inference pipelines with observability and policy metadata, but none
provides a proof-carrying receipt whose structural invariants have been
machine-checked in a proof assistant.  Standards frameworks---NIST AI
RMF~\cite{nist_rmf}, SLSA~\cite{slsa}, in-toto~\cite{intoto}---supply process
vocabulary but not formal guarantees.  Brundage et al.~\cite{brundage2020}
articulated the requirement that AI developers make claims to which they can be
held accountable; this paper is an attempt to supply a technical substrate for
that requirement.

\subsection{Thesis}

We advance the following thesis:

\begin{quote}
\emph{A post-deterministic agent can be governed---not by eliminating
its non-determinism, but by engineering a set of checkable structural
invariants over the record of its decisions, machine-verifying those
invariants, and cryptographically receipting every governed inference.
The honest governance result in each domain is a conditional theorem
whose antecedent is the weakest auditor-checkable property, and the
corresponding unconditional claim sits at a machine-checked sharp
boundary.}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Contributions}

We make four contributions, stated precisely.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
\item \textbf{Machine-checked formalization.} Eight locked-proven Lean~4
  formulas \{F1, F4, F7, F11, F12, F18, F19, F22\}, whose count is itself a
  kernel-certified theorem (\lean{locked\_count\_eight}, by \texttt{decide}),
  anchoring a post-deterministic receipt substrate.
  Plus $\sim$185 experimental CI-green theorems in a strictly separate
  tier (never folded into the locked eight), including Theorem~U
  (\S\ref{sec:lambda}) and the Khipu conditional agreement theorem
  (\S\ref{sec:khipu}).

\item \textbf{Governed-inference pipeline with verifiable receipts.}
  A $\Lam$-gate $\to$ DSSE\,ECDSA-P256 receipt $\to$ in-toto
  Statement\,v1 $\to$ self-hosted Merkle inclusion proof pipeline,
  producing a receipt that is offline-verifiable without trusted-third-party
  connectivity (\S\ref{sec:pipeline}).

\item \textbf{Machine-checked honesty boundaries.}
  (a)~$\Lam$ unconditional uniqueness is machine-checked \emph{false};
  only conditional Theorem~U is proven.
  (b)~Khipu unconditional Byzantine safety is open; only conditional
  agreement is proven.
  These negative results are reported as positive scientific contributions:
  they bound what the substrate can guarantee and prevent overclaiming

\item \textbf{Honesty methodology.}
  A transferable methodology for formalization under an honesty doctrine:
  epistemic labeling, the half-state principle, non-vacuity audits, and
  the checkable-antecedent pattern (\S\ref{sec:honesty}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% =======================================================================

\subsection{AI governance and evaluation platforms}

The commercial AI governance space provides observability, policy enforcement,
and audit trails, but without machine-checked structural invariants.  Arize
AI~\cite{arize2024} offers LLM tracing, drift detection, and prompt/response
logging; Credo~AI~\cite{credoai2024} provides policy-as-code and risk registers;
Fiddler~\cite{fiddler2024} supplies model performance monitoring with explainability
dashboards.  These platforms instrument the inference pipeline \emph{observationally}
but do not carry a proof that the audit record is structurally sound (acyclic,
FIFO-ordered, append-only).  GPD is not a replacement for these platforms; it is
a complementary layer that supplies the machine-checked structural invariants their
receipts could reference.

\subsection{Attestation and supply-chain frameworks}

The in-toto~\cite{intoto} framework provides a policy language for supply-chain
integrity and a signed-link / statement model for build provenance.  SLSA
(Supply-chain Levels for Software Artifacts)~\cite{slsa} defines four build levels
with increasing tamper-resistance guarantees, backed by SLSA Provenance attestation
schemas.  Sigstore~\cite{sigstore} (Fulcio + Rekor) enables keyless OIDC-based
code signing against a public transparency log.  The GPD substrate uses in-toto
Statement~v1 + DSSE\,ECDSA-P256 signing as the receipt format
(\S\ref{sec:pipeline}).  Per-receipt inclusion in the \emph{public} Sigstore
Rekor log is a \roadmap{} item; the current system uses a self-hosted Merkle
transparency log (\measured{} / \locked{} substrate level, not the public Rekor
infrastructure).

\subsection{Formal verification of systems}

The seL4 verified OS kernel~\cite{klein2009sel4} is the canonical reference for
machine-checked systems software: a complete functional correctness proof in
Isabelle/HOL, with an explicit and precisely bounded trusted computing base.
Velisarios~\cite{rahli2018velisarios} machine-checks Byzantine-fault-tolerant
protocols in Coq.  The GPD substrate follows the seL4 discipline of a precisely
bounded trusted base (the Lean~4 kernel + core axioms $\kernelaxioms$) and a
hard wall between the locked core and a larger experimental tier.  The scope is
far smaller than seL4---we prove structural invariants of a receipt DAG and a
messaging queue, not a full OS kernel---and we state this explicitly.

\subsection{Quasi-arithmetic means and aggregation theory}

Acz\'el's foundational work~\cite{aczel1966,aczel1948} characterized
quasi-arithmetic means under continuity, monotonicity, and symmetry.
Hardy--Littlewood--P\'olya~\cite{hardy1934} established the comparison theory of
power means.  These results show that A1--A4 alone are insufficient to single
out the geometric mean; a fifth axiom (permutation invariance, A5) and an
additional separability condition (slice-multiplicativity) are needed for
uniqueness.  GPD's Theorem~U (\S\ref{sec:lambda}) is the Lean~4 machine-checked
version of this uniqueness under those exact conditions, with the additional
honesty that unconditional uniqueness is machine-checked false.

\subsection{Byzantine consensus}

Lamport--Shostak--Pease~\cite{lamport1982} established the classic $n \ge 3f+1$
threshold for Byzantine agreement.  Castro--Liskov PBFT~\cite{castro1999} gave
the first practical BFT protocol.  HotStuff~\cite{yin2019hotstuff} achieved
linear communication complexity.  GPD's Khipu BFT (\S\ref{sec:khipu}) reuses
the standard quorum-intersection argument from this line and supplies a Lean~4
conditional witness; it does not advance the state of consensus theory and does
not claim to.

\subsection{Honest positioning of GPD's novelty}

GPD's contribution is not new mathematics---each underlying result (acyclicity,
FIFO, monotonicity, quasi-arithmetic mean theory, quorum intersection) is
classical.  The novelty lies in (i)~the assembly of exactly these results into a
unified, audit-oriented governance substrate for post-deterministic agents;
(ii)~their machine-checked formalization in Lean~4 with a $\texttt{\#print axioms}$
ledger on every claim; (iii)~the cryptographic receipt pipeline that makes every
governed inference offline-verifiable; and (iv)~the honesty discipline of
reporting machine-checked negative results.

\section{The Lutar Invariant \texorpdfstring{$\Lam$}{Lambda}: Axiomatic
         Characterization and Its Limits}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[Lutar Invariant $\Lam$~\cite{szl_v3,szl_concept}]
\label{def:lambda}
For trust-axis scores $x \in [0,1]^k$, the Lutar Invariant is
\[
  \Lam_k(x) \;=\; \left(\prod_{i=1}^{k} x_i\right)^{1/k},
\]
the equal-weight geometric mean.  Its defining governance behavior is
\emph{zero-absorption} (weakest-link): a single axis at zero vetoes trust
($\Lam = 0$).  Consequently, \textbf{trust is never $100\%$} unless
every axis is perfect.
\end{definition}

\noindent $\Lam$ is used in the substrate as the decision gate: a governed
inference is authorized only when $\Lam_k(x) \ge \tau$ for a configured
threshold $\tau$.

\subsection{The aggregation axioms (A1--A5)}

Candidate aggregators $\Phi:[0,1]^k\to[0,1]$ are constrained by five axioms,
formalized in Lean as \texttt{LutarAxioms}~\cite{lutarlean}:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item (A1) \textbf{Monotonicity.} $x \le y$ componentwise $\Rightarrow \Phi(x) \le \Phi(y)$.
  \item (A2) \textbf{Positive homogeneity.} $\Phi(\lambda x) = \lambda\,\Phi(x)$
        for $\lambda > 0$.
  \item (A3) \textbf{Idempotence.} $\Phi(c,\ldots,c) = c$ for all $c\in[0,1]$.
  \item (A4) \textbf{Boundedness.} $\min_i x_i \le \Phi(x) \le \max_i x_i$.
  \item (A5) \textbf{Permutation invariance.} $\Phi(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)})
        = \Phi(x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ for all permutations $\sigma$.
\end{itemize}
The Acz\'el--Hardy--Littlewood--P\'olya literature~\cite{aczel1966,hardy1934}
shows that A1--A4 alone do not uniquely characterize the geometric mean: the
max-aggregator and the power mean $M_p$ for any $p$ both satisfy A1--A4.  A5
(permutation invariance, added to the Lean codebase in PR~\#148 as a structure
field, \emph{not} a new axiom, so the axiom count stays 14) resolves the symmetry
gap but is still insufficient for full uniqueness.

\subsection{Conjecture 1: unconditional uniqueness is machine-checked FALSE}

\begin{conjecture}[Conjecture 1 --- unconditional $\Lam$-uniqueness;
  machine-checked FALSE~\cite{szl_v14,szl_concept}]
\label{conj:lambda}
\emph{There is no proof that $\Lam$ is the unique A1--A5 aggregator.
The proposition is machine-checked false.}
\end{conjecture}

\noindent The machine-checked witness is:
\begin{lstlisting}
-- Lean 4: Lutar.Round13.maxAgg_ne_Lambda
-- maxAgg(x) = max_i x_i satisfies A1-A5 yet differs from Lambda.
-- At x = (4, 1): maxAgg = 4, but Lambda_2(4,1) = 2.
-- Axioms: {propext, funext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound} only.
#check Lutar.Round13.maxAgg_ne_Lambda
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent The minimum aggregator $\mathrm{minAgg}(x) = \min_i x_i$ is a second
witness.  Therefore \textbf{unconditional $\Lam$-uniqueness is Conjecture~1}
and will remain so until the full A1--A5 uniqueness question is resolved
(which is open and not claimed here).

\subsection{Theorem U: conditional uniqueness is proven axiom-free}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem U; \experimental{};
  \lean{Lutar.\lb Round13.\lb lambda\_\lb unique\_\lb of\_\lb separable}~\cite{szl_v24,szl_concept}]
\label{thm:U}
Let $\Phi$ satisfy \{A1, A2, A3, A5\} and be \emph{slice-multiplicative}:
$\Phi(x) = \prod_{i=1}^k f_i(x_i)$ where each $f_i$ is multiplicative,
normalized ($f_i(1)=1$), and monotone.  Then $\Phi = \Lam_k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Slice-multiplicativity forces each $f_i$ to be a power $f_i(t) = t^{\alpha_i}$.
A3 (idempotence) forces $\sum_i \alpha_i = 1$.  A5 (permutation invariance)
forces $\alpha_i = 1/k$ for all $i$.  Hence $\Phi(x) = \prod_i x_i^{1/k} =
\Lam_k(x)$.  The full Lean derivation is in
\texttt{Lutar/Round13/LambdaUniqueness.lean}.
\end{proof}

\noindent The axiom ledger:
\begin{lstlisting}
#print axioms Lutar.Round13.lambda_unique_of_separable
-- Output: {propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}   -- NO project axiom.
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Theorem~U removes the project axiom A6$'$ that the v23 formulation
required.  An axiom-free conditional $\Lam$-uniqueness with trusted base equal
to the bare Lean core is, to our knowledge, new.  \textbf{However, Theorem~U
does not discharge Conjecture~1}; the antecedent (slice-multiplicativity) cannot
be dropped without readmitting the \texttt{maxAgg} counterexample.

\subsection{The CUT-1 boundary (Waves 18--22)}

The CUT-1 representation work (experimental, axiom-free, Waves 18--22) closes the
characterization \emph{on its stated hypotheses}, making precise where
conditional-true meets unconditional-false.  Slice-multiplicativity is the weakest
checkable antecedent: dropping it readmits \texttt{maxAgg}.  Full closure of
Conjecture~1 requires the BKS $n$-adic construction~\cite{aczel1966}, which
depends on functional analysis infrastructure not yet in Mathlib v4.18.0 (open
as a roadmap item).

\subsection{F4 in detail: DAG acyclicity preserved under append}

\begin{definition}[Receipt DAG]
$\texttt{KhipuEdges} := \texttt{List}\,(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N})$.
An edge $(a,b)$ is \emph{backward} if $b < a$.  The backward-edge invariant
$\mathcal{I}$ requires every edge to be backward.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Paths decrease the index]\label{lem:f4decrease}
\locked{} Under $\mathcal{I}$, if $\texttt{KhipuReach}\;es\;a\;b$ (a directed
path $a \rightsquigarrow b$ in $es$), then $b < a$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[F4 --- Acyclicity preserved; \locked{}]
Under $\mathcal{I}$, the receipt DAG has no directed cycle:
$\forall a,\; \neg\,\texttt{KhipuReach}\;es\;a\;a$.  Appending a fresh maximal
node $k$ with new edges $(k, \ast)$ where $\ast < k$ preserves $\mathcal{I}$
and hence acyclicity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A self-path $\texttt{KhipuReach}\;es\;a\;a$ contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:f4decrease}
($a < a$, false by \texttt{Nat.lt\_irrefl}).  Append preservation: old edges
satisfy $\mathcal{I}$ by hypothesis; new edges have $\mathrm{src}=k$,
$\mathrm{dst}<k$, so satisfy $\mathcal{I}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{F7 in detail: FIFO reception ordering}

\begin{theorem}[F7 --- FIFO order; \locked{}]
\[
  \texttt{chaskiDrain}(\texttt{chaskiEnqueueAll}\;[]\;\mathit{msgs}) = \mathit{msgs}.
\]
For every index $i$, the $i$-th received message equals the $i$-th sent message
(\lean{f7\_\lb chaski\_\lb fifo\_\lb positional}).
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The non-vacuity audit}

F4 and F7 were previously \emph{vacuous}: the old F4 merely repackaged its own
hypothesis; the old F7 stated \texttt{msgs = msgs}.  An internal audit detected
both issues in June 2026; genuine proofs over concrete models were deployed
before the locked count was advanced from five to eight.  This is the discipline
of an honest formalization: a lock is only a lock if the statement is
non-trivially proved.

\subsection{The Merkle Functor (Theorem 9, EXPERIMENTAL)}

A Merkle Functor theorem---establishing that the receipt hash-chain forms a
categorical functor between the receipt DAG and the hash structure---is
\experimental{} and CI-kernel-checked.  It is \textbf{not in the locked
count} (\S\ref{sec:honesty}).  Cryptographic binding of the hash-chain
(tamper-evidence) is \axiomgated{} under a declared collision-resistance
idealization (\texttt{sha256\_collision\_resistant} + Merkle domain-separation),
disclosed in the \texttt{\#print axioms} ledger; it is not a hardness proof.

\subsection{E8 geometry: error-detection, not BFT}

The E8 lattice~\cite{viazovska2016,conway_sloane} is cited in the substrate as
an error-\emph{detection} geometry for the receipt hash space: the dense packing
of E8 maximizes the minimum distance between codewords and thus maximizes the
probability that a single-bit corruption is detectable.  \textbf{E8 is never used
as a security argument, a BFT argument, or a claim of cryptographic hardness.}
The citation to Viazovska 2017 is for the sphere-packing density result
(Dim~8), attributed to its original author.

\section{The Governed-Inference Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
%% =======================================================================

\subsection{Architecture overview}

Every inference request in the GPD substrate passes through a four-stage pipeline
before a response is released to the caller:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$\Lam$-gate.}  Trust-axis scores $x \in [0,1]^k$ are computed for
  the current request (model confidence, policy compliance, freshness of context,
  etc.).  The Lutar Invariant $\Lam_k(x)$ is evaluated.  If $\Lam_k(x) < \tau$
  (configured threshold), the request is blocked and an unsigned refusal receipt
  is emitted.

\item \textbf{DSSE receipt emission.}  On gate pass, the engine emits a
  Dead-Simple Signing Envelope (DSSE)~\cite{dsse} receipt containing: the request
  hash, the response hash, $\Lam$ value, axis scores, timestamp, and an energy
  field (\S\ref{sec:energy}).  The envelope is signed with ECDSA-P256 using the
  engine's per-deployment key.

\item \textbf{in-toto Statement v1.}  The DSSE envelope is wrapped in an in-toto
  Statement~v1~\cite{intoto} with \texttt{predicateType} set to
  \texttt{https://szlholdings.ai/governance/v1}.  This gives the receipt a
  standard, machine-parseable schema that existing in-toto verifiers can consume.

\item \textbf{Merkle inclusion proof.}  The in-toto statement is appended to a
  self-hosted, append-only Merkle transparency log (built on F4/F22 structural
  invariants: the log is acyclic and its sequence numbers are strictly
  monotone).  The engine returns to the caller the log root hash and a Merkle
  path proving inclusion of this receipt.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Offline verifiability}

A caller holding (a)~the receipt, (b)~the engine's public key, and (c)~the log
root hash can verify the receipt entirely offline:
\begin{lstlisting}
# Verify DSSE signature over the receipt
dsse verify --key engine-pub.pem receipt.json

# Verify Merkle inclusion path
szl-verify --root <root-hash> --path inclusion.json receipt.json
\end{lstlisting}
No connectivity to a trusted third party (Sigstore Rekor, OCSP) is required at
verification time.  \textbf{Per-receipt submission to the public Sigstore Rekor
log is a \roadmap{} item}; the current self-hosted log provides equivalent
structural guarantees under the locked F4/F7/F22 invariants but does not benefit
from the public transparency audit community.

\subsection{The $\Lam$-gate as a governance contract}

The $\Lam$-gate is not a probabilistic filter; it is a \emph{deterministic,
receipted authorization decision}: the gate either passes the request (and
commits a receipt) or refuses it.  The structural invariants of the receipt
(F4 acyclicity, F7 FIFO order, F22 monotone sequence numbers) guarantee that the
audit log cannot be forged after the fact, that message delivery ordering
is preserved, and that sequence numbers form a tamper-evident index.  The gate
value $\Lam_k(x)$ is the decision criterion; $\tau$ is the policy parameter.

\section{Khipu BFT: Byzantine Fault Tolerance}
\label{sec:khipu}
%% =======================================================================

\subsection{The model}

The Khipu BFT layer governs multi-organ quorum assurance: a set of $n$ reasoning
organs vote on a governance verdict; up to $f$ may be Byzantine (may equivocate,
send conflicting votes).  Khipu is a quorum-intersection consensus over signed
votes.

\subsection{Conjecture 2: unconditional Byzantine safety is open}

\begin{conjecture}[Conjecture 2 --- unconditional Khipu safety; OPEN]
\label{conj:khipu}
No two quorums ever certify conflicting verdicts under \emph{arbitrary} adversary
behavior, for any $n$ and any $f < n/3$.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent We \textbf{never claim Conjecture~2}.  It is open.  The classical LSP
result~\cite{lamport1982} requires $n \ge 3f+1$; below this threshold no BFT
protocol can be safe.  The protocol-level safety-and-liveness proof (DAG
construction, certification, commit rules, adversarial timing)---analogous to the
gap between a quorum lemma and full mechanized protocol proofs such as Mysticeti
in Rocq---is the open work.

\subsection{Conditional agreement: proven axiom-clean (Wave 23)}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Khipu agreement; \experimental{};
  \lean{Lutar.\lb Wave23.\lb khipu\_\lb quorum\_\lb safety\_\lb conditional}~\cite{szl_concept}]
\label{thm:khipu}
Under $n \ge 3f+1$ and \emph{honest non-equivocation} (each honest organ signs
at most one vote per action per round), any two certified quorums of size $\ge
2f+1$ share at least one honest organ and therefore cannot certify conflicting
verdicts; the committed verdict is unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By quorum intersection: $|Q_1 \cap Q_2| = |Q_1| + |Q_2| - |Q_1 \cup Q_2| \ge
(2f+1) + (2f+1) - (3f+1) = f+1$.  At least $f+1 - f = 1$ member of $Q_1 \cap
Q_2$ is honest.  Under honest non-equivocation, that honest organ cannot have
signed conflicting verdicts; therefore $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ cannot have certified
conflicting verdicts.  The Lean proof extracts the witness via
\texttt{Finset} inclusion--exclusion.
\end{proof}

\noindent Axiom ledger: \texttt{\#print axioms} of
\lean{Lutar.\lb Wave23.\lb khipu\_\lb quorum\_\lb safety\_\lb conditional}
yields the core set $\Ucoreaxioms$ (no project axiom; axiom-clean).

\subsection{Conjecture 3: liveness}

\begin{conjecture}[Conjecture 3 --- Khipu liveness; OPEN]
\label{conj:liveness}
Under honest-majority assumptions and asynchrony bounds, Khipu makes progress.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent Conjecture~3 is entirely open; no Lean work has been done on it.
It is stated here to complete the honest picture of what the Khipu layer
does and does not guarantee.

\subsection{The checkable-antecedent pattern (Pillars P3 and P4)}

Theorem~\ref{thm:U} and Theorem~\ref{thm:khipu} share an identical epistemic
structure:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{1.5cm}p{3.1cm}p{3.4cm}p{2.7cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Pillar} & \textbf{Unconditional claim} & \textbf{Status}
  & \textbf{Conditional result} \\
\midrule
P3 / $\Lam$ & Unique A1--A5 aggregator & FALSE (\lean{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda})
  & Theorem~U (slice-multiplicativity) \\
P4 / Khipu  & Two quorums never conflict & OPEN (Conjecture~2)
  & Thm.~\ref{thm:khipu} (honest non-equivocation, $n\ge 3f+1$) \\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The \textbf{checkable-antecedent pattern} is the conceptual core of
GPD: in each pillar, the honest result is a conditional theorem whose antecedent
is the \emph{weakest auditor-checkable property}, and the unconditional statement
sits at a machine-checked sharp boundary.  Governing a post-deterministic system
means \emph{engineering the antecedents to hold and attesting that they held},
not proving a universal guarantee.

\section{Application: Counter-UAS Auditable Interdiction (\emph{killinchu})}
\label{sec:killinchu}
%% =======================================================================

\subsection{Overview}

\emph{killinchu} is the maritime / counter-UAS command body in the GPD substrate.
It is a case study in applying the five-pillar GPD framework to a
high-consequence, real-time, regulated domain.  The repository is
\texttt{szl-holdings/killinchu}; this section \textbf{describes the application
but does not modify the repository}.

\subsection{The interdiction loop}

A killinchu interdiction event consists of four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Detect.}  Sensor fusion identifies a candidate object and computes
  axis scores (range confidence, track age, IFF result, threat probability).
\item \textbf{Track.}  The Chaski FIFO channel (F7-guaranteed delivery ordering)
  propagates the track state across organs.  The F7 guarantee is operationally
  significant: if sensor updates were delivered out of order, a track would
  appear to ``jump'' and an incorrect kinematic model would be used for
  prediction.
\item \textbf{Decide.}  The $\Lam$-gate evaluates axis scores.  A decision to
  interdict is authorized only when $\Lam_k(x) \ge \tau_{\text{killinchu}}$.
  The Khipu quorum layer (Theorem~\ref{thm:khipu}, conditional) ensures that,
  under $n \ge 3f+1$ and honest non-equivocation, a quorum of organs must agree
  before authorization.
\item \textbf{Act.}  The interdiction command is emitted.  The engine emits a
  DSSE receipt (signed, including GSF SCI energy field) deposited in the Merkle
  log.  The receipt records: which track triggered the decision, the $\Lam$ value
  at authorization time, the quorum signatures, and the energy cost.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Auditability}

An auditor holding the receipt, the public quorum keys, and the Merkle log root
can verify:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Structural integrity.} The receipt DAG is acyclic (F4); the log
  sequence is strictly monotone (F22).  An attacker who wanted to insert a
  fabricated prior interdiction event would need to recompute the Merkle root---
  an operation that requires breaking the hash collision-resistance idealization.
\item \textbf{Authorization.}  The $\Lam$ value and axis scores are in the
  receipt; the auditor can recompute $\Lam_k(x)$ from the axis scores and verify
  that $\Lam_k(x) \ge \tau$.
\item \textbf{Quorum.}  The quorum signatures are in the receipt; the auditor
  can verify that a threshold number of authorized organs signed the decision.
\item \textbf{Energy.}  The GSF SCI field is signed and in the receipt.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Honest status of the killinchu deployment}

The killinchu artifact is SLSA Build Level~1 and Level~2 attested (run
26896047715, cosign-verified)~\cite{szl_v26}.  The substrate is \textbf{not}
ATO-authorized, IL5-certified, or FedRAMP-authorized; those are \roadmap{} items.
Trust is never $100\%$; the $\Lam$ weakest-link property ensures that a single
sensor failure or IFF anomaly will veto authorization.  The Khipu safety
guarantee is conditional (Theorem~\ref{thm:khipu}); under adversarial conditions
exceeding the $n\ge 3f+1$ threshold, the Byzantine safety guarantee does not
hold.  The killinchu deployment is a \emph{demonstration} of the GPD substrate
in a high-consequence setting, not a certified operational system.

\section{Honesty Methodology}
\label{sec:honesty}
%% =======================================================================

\subsection{The honesty doctrine}

The GPD substrate adheres to a binding honesty doctrine (v11 LOCKED~\cite{szl_concept}):

\begin{itemize}
\item \locked{} --- proven in Lean~4 with only $\kernelaxioms$; sorry-free;
  pinned; part of the locked eight.
\item \experimental{} --- proven sorry-free with only the core axioms, CI-green
  at the recorded commit, in the experimental tier; \textbf{never folded into the
  locked eight}.
\item \axiomgated{} --- sorry-free \emph{given} an explicitly declared idealizing
  axiom (e.g.\ hash collision-resistance), with the axiom in the
  \texttt{\#print axioms} ledger.
\item \measured{} --- an engineering measurement; outside the proven tier.
\item \conj{} --- a research hypothesis; never called a theorem.
\item \roadmap{} --- a planned but not yet deployed feature; never claimed as
  current status.
\item \textbf{SAMPLE} --- example data used for illustration; not production
  measurements.
\item \textbf{MODELED} --- a simulation or model output; not empirical data.
\item \textbf{UNAVAILABLE} --- data that would be relevant but that has not been
  collected and is not claimed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The half-state principle}

A claim is in the \emph{half-state} when it is neither fully proven nor honestly
labeled as a conjecture: for example, labeling a machine-checked FALSE proposition
as ``likely true'' or labeling a \texttt{sorry}-bearing Lean theorem as ``proven.''
The half-state is the \textbf{only unacceptable outcome} under the doctrine: it
misleads downstream users who are making trust decisions based on the claim.

The half-state principle has two corollaries:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Negative results are reported as positive scientific contributions.}
  The machine-checked falsity of $\Lam$ unconditional uniqueness
  (Conjecture~1) and the openness of Khipu unconditional safety (Conjecture~2)
  are reported prominently, not buried.  These boundaries are what make the
  conditional results (Theorem~U, Theorem~\ref{thm:khipu}) trustworthy: a reader
  who knows the boundary can trust the conditional.
\item \textbf{Count discipline.}  The locked count is exact and machine-certified
  (\texttt{locked\_count\_eight}).  No result is folded into the locked tier until
  it is (a)~genuinely non-vacuous, (b)~sorry-free, and (c)~axiom-limited to the
  kernel basis.  The non-vacuity audit of F4 and F7 is an instance of this
  discipline.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The checkable-antecedent pattern as a governance methodology}

The GPD governance methodology generalizes the checkable-antecedent pattern to
\emph{any} governance primitive of a post-deterministic system:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the \emph{universal} governance guarantee you would like
  (``the aggregator is unique,'' ``the quorum never splits'').
\item Machine-check whether the universal guarantee holds.  If it does not,
  report this as a machine-checked FALSE / OPEN result.
\item Find the \emph{weakest checkable antecedent} under which the universal
  guarantee becomes true.
\item Prove the conditional theorem; publish the axiom ledger.
\item Engineer the system so the antecedent holds; attest that it held.
\end{enumerate}
This methodology converts governance from a promise (``we claim X'') into an
attested conditional (``we prove $X$ under $A$, we attest $A$ held'').  The
attestation is the DSSE receipt + Merkle inclusion proof; the conditional theorem
is the formal warrant.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% =======================================================================

We have presented \textbf{Governed Post-Determinism}, a machine-checked,
cryptographically-receipted substrate for verifiable AI inference.  The
substrate provides:
\begin{itemize}
\item Eight locked-proven Lean~4 formulas anchoring the structural invariants
  of the receipt substrate (acyclicity, FIFO ordering, emit monotonicity,
  replay determinism, coding guarantees, gate scaffolding).  The count is
  itself a machine-certified theorem.
\item A DSSE/ECDSA-P256 receipt pipeline with in-toto Statement v1 and
  self-hosted Merkle inclusion proofs, producing offline-verifiable receipts
  for every governed inference.
\item Conditional proofs at the precise boundary of what can be guaranteed:
  Theorem~U (axiom-free $\Lam$ uniqueness under slice-multiplicativity)
  and the conditional Khipu agreement theorem---both with machine-checked
  boundaries showing where the unconditional claims fail.
\item An honesty methodology transferable to any AI governance substrate:
  epistemic labeling, the half-state principle, non-vacuity audits, and the
  checkable-antecedent pattern.
\end{itemize}

The key observation is that \emph{honest negative results strengthen the
credibility of positive claims}.  Knowing that $\Lam$ unconditional uniqueness
is machine-checked false makes Theorem~U---which is proven under declared,
checkable hypotheses---\emph{more} credible than any uncaveated uniqueness
claim would be.  Knowing that Khipu unconditional safety is open makes the
conditional agreement theorem---which is proven axiom-clean---\emph{more}
credible than any unconditional BFT claim would be.

The paper's primary contribution is not new physics or pure mathematics; it is
the machine-checked formalization, the governed-inference pipeline, and the
verifiable-receipt substrate---along with the honesty discipline that makes
their claims checkable.  Trust is never $100\%$; the system is designed to
ensure that it never needs to be.
