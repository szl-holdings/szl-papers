\begin{abstract}
Governed AI systems increasingly represent their internal state as a graph: a
mesh of organs that aggregate trust, exchange messages, and emit provenance.
We ask which \emph{graph-structural} guarantees of such a substrate can be made
machine-checked, and we deliver eleven \texttt{sorry}-free Lean~4 theorems that
do so, every one depending on no axiom beyond the Lean core
$\kernelaxioms$ (several are even axiom-free). Our results are organized around
four guarantees a governed graph substrate ought to provide and we prove each in
its honest scope: (F-G4) the graph-lifted trust aggregator
$\Lgraph$ --- a per-vertex equal-weight geometric mean composed over the vertex
set --- is \emph{invariant under graph automorphism and isomorphism}, so a
relabelling of organs cannot change the global verdict; (F-G2) any
message-passing graph neural network (GNN) whose readout factors through the
Weisfeiler--Lehman (WL) colour-refinement map can distinguish no more than
$1$-WL, the honest expressivity \emph{upper bound}; (F-G3) a one-step contraction
toward a stationary point by factor $\lambda\in[0,1)$ yields the geometric
mixing bound $\mathrm{dist}(P^{[t]}x,\pi)\leq\lambda^{t}\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\pi)$;
and (F-G5) a frontier-draining walk over a finite receipt DAG terminates in a
bounded number of steps. We also prove the per-anchor non-expansion certificate
(F-G1) underlying P-GNN-style distance embeddings, and relabelling invariance of
adjacency-derived functionals (F-G6). We engage and cite the graph-learning
lineage that informs this work --- position-aware GNNs and the GraphGym/SNAP
design-space program, the relational-graph view of network structure, the
$1$-WL ceiling, scientific machine learning, and recurrent-depth reasoning ---
as related and prior art, never as a source of our theorems. We are explicit
about scope: F-G1 is the finite/fixed-anchor expansion-side core, not the full
$O(\log n)$-distortion asymptotic; F-G3 is the contraction-bound core, not a
full mixing-time theorem; and the trust aggregator's unconditional uniqueness
remains \textbf{Conjecture~1} (machine-checked false as stated). The locked
Doctrine-v11 kernel ($749/14/163$) is unchanged; all results are additive in the
experimental scope.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} graph neural networks, Weisfeiler--Lehman,
expressivity, isomorphism invariance, geometric mean, spectral contraction,

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A governed AI substrate is naturally a graph. In the SZL ``living anatomy''
architecture, organs (reasoning, policy, provenance, immune) are vertices; their
trust relationships and message channels are edges; and a global ``verdict'' is
computed by aggregating per-organ trust across the graph. Once state is a graph,
three families of questions become unavoidable and \emph{governance-critical}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Invariance.} Does the global verdict depend on \emph{how the organs
  are labelled}? If a mere relabelling of vertices changed the verdict, the
  system would be non-deterministic in a way no auditor could accept. We want
  the aggregator to be a graph \emph{invariant}.
\item \textbf{Expressivity, honestly bounded.} If the substrate uses
  message-passing to summarize structure, what can it provably \emph{not}
  distinguish? Overstating a model's discriminative power is a governance risk;
  we want an honest \emph{upper bound}.
\item \textbf{Stability and termination.} Does the iterative aggregation
  contract toward a fixed point, and does the audit walk over the receipt DAG
  terminate? Without these, ``governed recursion'' is a promise, not a property.
\end{enumerate}

This paper makes each of these a machine-checked theorem in Lean~4. The
guarantees are organized so that each maps to a concern an auditor of a graph-
structured AI system would raise, and each is proven in a precisely stated
scope. We do not invent new graph theory; the value is in (i) selecting the
\emph{right} small set of guarantees for a governance substrate, (ii) proving
them with no custom axiom, and (iii) being scrupulous about the gap between what
is proven and the asymptotic statements the literature would suggest.

\paragraph{The lineage we engage.} Our thinking about graph-structured learning
is shaped by a specific body of work that we cite as related and prior art and
never claim as our own: \emph{position-aware} graph neural networks, which break
the $1$-WL ceiling by anchoring on distances~\cite{you2019pgnn}; the
GraphGym/SNAP \emph{design space} program, which treats GNN architecture as a
searchable space~\cite{you2020graphgym}; the relational-graph view that a neural
network's wiring is itself a graph whose structure predicts
performance~\cite{you2020graph2nn}; the foundational result that message-passing
GNNs are at most as powerful as $1$-WL~\cite{xu2019gin}; scientific machine
learning across multiple physics~\cite{mccabe2023multiphysics}; and
recurrent-depth / reasoning-by-iteration~\cite{mcleish2024abacus}. Our substrate
(this paper) connects to that lineage by formalizing exactly the structural
invariants those works rely on or bound --- isomorphism invariance, the $1$-WL
ceiling, distance-embedding non-expansion, and contraction --- and by doing so
in a kernel-checked setting suitable for governance audit.

\section{F-G4 --- The graph-lifted aggregator is an isomorphism invariant}\label{sec:fg4}

\subsection{Definition}

\begin{definition}[Graph-lifted $\Lambda$]
For a finite graph with vertex set $V$ and a per-vertex score $s : V \to [0,1]$,
the per-vertex aggregate $\texttt{vertexLambda}(v)$ is the equal-weight geometric
mean over $v$'s closed neighbourhood, and the graph-level aggregate is
$\Lgraph := \big(\prod_{v\in V}\texttt{vertexLambda}(v)\big)^{1/|V|}$, the
geometric mean of the per-vertex aggregates.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Automorphism / isomorphism]
A \texttt{LambdaAutomorphism} is an edge- and score-preserving vertex bijection
$\sigma : V \to V$. A \texttt{LambdaIso} is the analogous score- and
edge-preserving bijection between two graphs.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Invariance theorems}

\begin{theorem}[Per-vertex invariance]\label{thm:vertinv}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{vertexLambda\_automorphism\_invariant}, axioms
$\kernelaxioms$]} For every \texttt{LambdaAutomorphism} $\sigma$ and vertex $v$,
$\texttt{vertexLambda}(\sigma v) = \texttt{vertexLambda}(v)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Graph-level invariance]\label{thm:graphinv}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{$\Lambda$\_graph\_automorphism\_invariant}]} The
graph-level aggregate $\Lgraph$ is invariant under any
\texttt{LambdaAutomorphism}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Cross-graph isomorphism invariance]\label{thm:isoinv}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{$\Lambda$\_graph\_iso\_invariant}]} If two finite
graphs are related by a \texttt{LambdaIso}, then their $\Lgraph$ values are
equal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Theorem~\ref{thm:vertinv} holds because $\sigma$ preserves edges and scores, so
the multiset of neighbourhood scores --- hence the per-vertex geometric mean ---
is preserved. Theorem~\ref{thm:graphinv} reindexes the product over $V$ by the
bijection $\sigma$ (\texttt{Finset.prod} is invariant under reindexing).
Theorem~\ref{thm:isoinv} uses \texttt{Fintype.prod\_equiv} to transport the
product across the two vertex sets and \texttt{Fintype.card\_congr} for equal
cardinality, so the two normalized products coincide. All proofs are
\texttt{sorry}-free with axioms $\kernelaxioms$; they replace a prior placeholder
\texttt{graph\_automorphism\_invariance\_tracked} with genuine content. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why this matters for governance.} Theorem~\ref{thm:isoinv} is the
formal guarantee that the global trust verdict is a property of the
\emph{structure} of the organ mesh, not of the arbitrary names assigned to
organs. It directly realizes the invariance concern of \S\ref{sec:intro}, and it
is the graph analogue of permutation-invariance for the flat aggregator. The
construction is informed by the relational-graph and position-aware GNN
lineage~\cite{you2020graph2nn,you2019pgnn}, which we cite as prior art.

\section{F-G2 --- An honest $1$-WL expressivity ceiling}\label{sec:fg2}

\begin{theorem}[GNN $\leq$ $1$-WL]\label{thm:gnnwl}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{gnn\_le\_wl}, axiom-free]} Any message-passing GNN
colouring that factors through the WL colour-refinement multiset map can
distinguish no more than $1$-WL: if two vertices receive equal WL colours, they
receive equal GNN representations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
If the GNN readout factors as $g \circ \mathrm{WL}$ for some function $g$, then
$\mathrm{WL}(u)=\mathrm{WL}(v)$ forces $g(\mathrm{WL}(u))=g(\mathrm{WL}(v))$, so
the GNN cannot separate what WL cannot. The proof is purely functional and uses
no axioms (bare \texttt{lean} reports ``does not depend on any axioms''). \qed
\end{proof}

This is deliberately stated as an \emph{upper} bound. We do not claim our
substrate's message passing is maximally expressive; we prove it cannot exceed
$1$-WL, the honest ceiling established for message-passing
GNNs~\cite{xu2019gin}. Where strictly-stronger-than-$1$-WL discrimination is
needed, the substrate must add position/distance features --- exactly the P-GNN
route~\cite{you2019pgnn} whose embedding core we formalize next.

\section{F-G1 --- Non-expansion of the distance embedding (finite core)}\label{sec:fg1}

\begin{theorem}[Per-anchor non-expansion]\label{thm:nonexpand}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{frechet\_coord\_lipschitz}, axioms
$\kernelaxioms$]} For any anchor $a$, the map $x \mapsto \mathrm{dist}(x,a)$
satisfies $|\mathrm{dist}(x,a)-\mathrm{dist}(y,a)| \leq \mathrm{dist}(x,y)$; it is
$1$-Lipschitz.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Isometric embedding into $\ell^\infty$]\label{thm:kuratowski}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{frechet\_isometric\_embedding}]} Every separable
metric space embeds isometrically (distortion $1$) into $\ell^\infty(\mathbb{N})$,
re-exporting Mathlib's Kuratowski embedding isometry.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Honest scope.} These are the \emph{expansion-side} core of any
Bourgain-style random-anchor construction: the per-anchor coordinate is
$1$-Lipschitz, so the stacked anchor-distance map does not expand distances.
This is the certificate a P-GNN's distance embedding needs on the
non-expansion side. We do \emph{not} prove the full $O(\log n)$-distortion
asymptotic of Bourgain's theorem~\cite{bourgain1985,llr1995}; that requires the
random-anchor lower bound, which we leave open and label as such.

\section{F-G3 --- Geometric spectral contraction}\label{sec:fg3}

\begin{theorem}[Geometric contraction]\label{thm:contraction}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{geometric\_contraction}, axioms $\kernelaxioms$]} If
a one-step map contracts toward a stationary point $\pi$ by a factor
$\lambda\in[0,1)$ --- $\mathrm{dist}(Px,\pi)\leq\lambda\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\pi)$ ---
then $\mathrm{dist}(P^{[t]}x,\pi)\leq\lambda^{t}\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\pi)$ for all
$t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Non-increase at $\lambda\leq 1$]\label{thm:nonincrease}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{contraction\_nonincrease}]} When $\lambda\leq 1$, a
single step never increases the distance to $\pi$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof idea]
Induction on $t$: the base case is the one-step hypothesis; the inductive step
multiplies the bound by $\lambda$. This promotes a prior toy inequality
($\text{gap}>0 \Rightarrow \text{stable}$) to a genuine Banach-style
geometric-mixing bound valid in any pseudometric space. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Honest scope.} This is the contraction-bound core, \emph{not} the
full reversible-chain mixing-time theorem; we cite the mixing-time
literature~\cite{levinperes2017,diaconisstroock1991} as the asymptotic context
we do not claim. The contraction bound is exactly what a governed iterative
aggregator (deep-equilibrium / recurrent-depth style~\cite{mcleish2024abacus})
needs to guarantee its fixed point is well-posed.

\section{F-G5 / F-G6 --- Bounded-frontier termination and relabelling invariance}\label{sec:fg5}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-frontier DAG termination]\label{thm:terminate}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{iterStep\_drains}, \texttt{iterStep\_empties},
\texttt{step\_lt}; axiom-free / \texttt{[propext]}]} A frontier-draining
iteration over a finite receipt DAG strictly decreases a well-founded
$\mathbb{N}$ measure each step and reaches the empty frontier in a bounded number
of steps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Relabelling-invariant functionals]\label{thm:relabel}
\emph{[experimental, \texttt{adj\_pred\_relabel\_invariant},
\texttt{countAdj\_relabel\_invariant}; axiom-free / \texttt{[propext]}]}
Adjacency-derived counts are invariant under a vertex relabelling (permutation),
the discrete core underpinning degree- and clustering-style functionals being
isomorphism invariants.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:terminate} is the termination/availability certificate for the
bounded-frontier audit walk: the graph traversal that reconstructs provenance
provably halts. Theorem~\ref{thm:relabel} complements F-G4 by certifying that the
\emph{descriptive} graph statistics (not just the aggregator) are invariants.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Our graph substrate sits squarely within, and is positioned against, the graph
representation-learning lineage. The $1$-WL ceiling of message-passing GNNs is
due to~\cite{xu2019gin}; the position-aware route that breaks it via anchor
distances is P-GNN~\cite{you2019pgnn}, whose embedding non-expansion we
formalize (\S\ref{sec:fg1}); the design-space view of GNN
architecture~\cite{you2020graphgym} and the relational-graph structure of neural
networks~\cite{you2020graph2nn} motivate treating the governance substrate
itself as a searchable, structured graph. Metric-embedding distortion bounds are
Bourgain's theorem and its algorithmic
form~\cite{bourgain1985,llr1995}; mixing-time / contraction theory
is~\cite{levinperes2017,diaconisstroock1991}; iterative-depth reasoning
is~\cite{mcleish2024abacus}; and scientific ML at scale is the Polymathic AI
program~\cite{mccabe2023multiphysics}. We engage all of these as prior art that
informs our framing; \emph{none} is claimed as a source of the theorems above,
which are kernel-checked statements in our own development. The discipline of a
small, machine-checked trusted base is the seL4 tradition~\cite{sel4}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item F-G1 is the finite/fixed-anchor expansion-side core; the $O(\log n)$
  distortion asymptotic is \emph{not} proven.
\item F-G3 is the contraction-bound core; the full mixing-time theorem is
  \emph{not} proven.
\item F-G2 is an \emph{upper} bound; it does not assert our substrate achieves
  $1$-WL, only that message passing cannot exceed it.
\item The flat trust aggregator's \emph{unconditional} uniqueness remains
  \textbf{Conjecture~1} (machine-checked false as stated); the graph lift inherits
  this. Only invariance (this paper) and conditional uniqueness (companion work)
  are proven~\cite{ouroboros25}.
\item Three planned information-theoretic targets (Hoeffding, Azuma,
  $\mathrm{KL}\geq 0$) are \textbf{deferred}: the required Mathlib modules do not
  exist at the pinned version, and we decline to bump Mathlib at the risk of the
  locked kernel. We report this rather than ship brittle proofs.
\item All results are experimental-tier and additive; the locked Doctrine-v11
  kernel ($749/14/163$) is unchanged. No fabricated benchmarks or citations;
  trust never reported as $100\%$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have given eleven \texttt{sorry}-free, axiom-disclosed Lean~4 theorems that
furnish a governed AI system with a kernel-verified graph substrate:
isomorphism-invariant trust aggregation, an honest $1$-WL expressivity ceiling, a
distance-embedding non-expansion certificate, a geometric spectral-contraction
bound, and bounded-frontier DAG termination. The contribution is not new graph
theory but the disciplined selection and machine-checked formalization of exactly
the structural guarantees a governance substrate needs, with every scope
boundary stated honestly and the harder asymptotics left explicitly open. The
work connects the SZL substrate to the graph representation-learning lineage it
draws on, as prior art and never as a source.
