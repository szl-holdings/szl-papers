\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Governed deployment of agentic artificial intelligence in regulated and defense
settings requires more than benchmark accuracy: it requires \emph{checkable}
guarantees about what a system did, why it was permitted to do it, and whether the
record can be tampered with after the fact. We present the \textbf{Unified
Substrate} (v23), the consolidation of twenty-two prior thesis versions (v1--v22)
of SZL Holdings into a single coherent account of a machine-verified trust
substrate for governed AI. The substrate rests on three pillars: (i) the
\emph{Ouroboros loop}, a bounded, well-founded self-governing computation in which
``the loop is the product''; (ii) the \emph{Lutar invariant} $\La_k(x) = \big(\prod_{i=1}^k
x_i\big)^{1/k}$, an equal-weight geometric-mean trust aggregator; and (iii) a
\emph{proof-trail / receipt architecture} whose integrity properties are
formalized in Lean~4. Our central methodological commitment is an
\textbf{honesty doctrine}: we distinguish, at every point of assertion, among
\emph{proven} (kernel-verified, Lean-core axioms only), \emph{proven under a
declared axiom} (idealization disclosed in the \printax{} ledger), and
\emph{conjectured} (a research hypothesis, not a theorem). Under that doctrine we
report: a \emph{locked kernel} proving exactly five governance formulas
($\{$F1, F11, F12, F18, F19$\}$, 749 declarations / 14 unique axioms / 163 sorries
at a fixed commit); \textbf{nineteen} additional sorry-free theorems (Kraft,
Shannon tight case, Byzantine $3f{+}1$ quorums, DLS partial-synchrony, the FLP
bivalence core, BLUE minimum-variance, and softmax order-stability) verified under
bare Lean with only the trusted core axioms; \textbf{four} axiom-gated Merkle /
Merkle--Damg{\aa}rd theorems under explicitly declared collision-resistance
idealizations; and a \emph{conditional} uniqueness theorem for $\La$ that is
CI-green under $\{$A1--A5$\}$ plus a single declared, disclosed,
governance-natural axiom (block-consistency, after Cs\'at\'o 2018). Crucially, the
\emph{unconditional} uniqueness of $\La$ under $\{$A1--A5$\}$ is provably
\textbf{false}: we exhibit a machine-checked counterexample. We therefore label
$\La$ as \textbf{Conjecture~1} unconditionally --- never a theorem --- and argue,
on grounds drawn from philosophy of mathematics and formal epistemology, that this
calibrated labeling is not a weakness but the very feature that makes the positive
claims credible to an auditor. We close with an honest comparison to prior art, a
philosophical defense of block-consistency as the right governance axiom, and an
explicit limitations section.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: the problem of governed AI}
\label{sec:intro}

Agentic AI systems --- models that plan, call tools, retrieve, and act over
multiple steps --- are entering domains where the cost of an undetected error is
not a bad recommendation but a regulatory breach, a safety incident, or a
compromised mission. In such regulated and defense settings the operative question
is no longer ``how accurate is the model on a benchmark?'' but ``can the operator
\emph{prove}, after the fact and to a skeptical third party, that a given action
was authorized, that the authorization rested on checkable evidence, and that the
record of what happened has not been altered?'' This is the problem of
\emph{governed AI}: the construction of an execution substrate in which trust is
\emph{earned by checkable evidence} rather than asserted by a vendor.

The dominant industry posture is the opposite. Trust is typically asserted
(``our model is safe''), the evidence is a private evaluation suite, and the audit
trail --- when it exists --- is a mutable log. The verifiable-claims research
program in AI governance argues that this is structurally inadequate: developers
should be able to make \emph{verifiable claims} about their systems, supported by
mechanisms an external party can check \cite{brundage2020}. National policy bodies
have echoed the same line, asking that developers be able to \emph{prove}
properties of their systems rather than be taken at their word \cite{ntia}.

\paragraph{Thesis.}
This document argues that the right response is a \emph{trust substrate} with three
mutually reinforcing properties, and that such a substrate can be built and
\emph{partially machine-verified} today with complete honesty about what is and is
not proven. The three properties are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{A bounded self-governing loop} (the Ouroboros loop,
    \S\ref{sec:loop}): governance is not a bolt-on filter but the recurrent
    structure of the computation itself, made safe by well-foundedness.
  \item \textbf{A principled trust aggregator} (the Lutar invariant $\La$,
    \S\ref{sec:lambda}): multi-axis trust evidence is combined into a single
    governance scalar by the equal-weight geometric mean, whose uniqueness ---
    \emph{conditional} on a disclosed governance axiom --- is machine-checked.
  \item \textbf{A tamper-evident proof trail} (the receipt architecture,
    \S\ref{sec:receipts}): every governed action emits a hash-chained,
    Merkle-bound, replayable receipt whose integrity properties are formalized in
    Lean~4.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is genuinely new.}
The contribution of v23 is not any single theorem but the \emph{unification},
under one honesty doctrine, of a trust substrate whose every claim carries an
explicit epistemic status disclosed at the point of use. To our knowledge this is
the first governed-AI trust substrate that (a) ships a \emph{locked},
kernel-verified core of governance formulas; (b) accompanies its central
uniqueness claim with a \emph{machine-checked refutation of the overclaim} (the
unconditional statement is \emph{false}), so that the conditional theorem is the
\emph{maximal true statement}; and (c) discloses every idealizing axiom in a
\printax{} ledger. \S\ref{sec:groundbreaking} makes the honest case for why this is
groundbreaking, and \S\ref{sec:limits} states plainly what it is not.

\paragraph{Reading guide and provenance.}
The substrate has a documented intellectual lineage of twenty-two versions
(\S\ref{sec:lineage}), archived under a concept DOI with per-version DOIs. Version
19 was intentionally skipped during a late-stage consolidation; the numbering jumps
v18\,$\to$\,v20, and this is documented rather than a missing artifact. v23 is the
unification of that lineage with all proofs that are currently kernel-verified or
CI-green per the proof reports of the formal-methods team.

\section{The Ouroboros loop thesis}
\label{sec:loop}

\subsection{The loop is the product}
The founding observation (v1--v2) is that a governed agentic system is best
understood not as a model with a governance wrapper but as a \emph{loop}: perceive,
propose, gate, act, record, and feed the record back into the next perception.
Governance is the recurrence relation, not a post-hoc filter. The slogan ``the loop
is the product'' means that the artifact a customer buys is the \emph{governed
recurrence}, with its receipts, not the underlying model weights.

\subsection{Why the loop is safe: well-foundedness, not self-justification}
A self-referential governing loop invites two classical worries: infinite regress
(the loop never halts) and self-verification paradox (a system proving its own
correctness). We address both \emph{by construction}, and we are careful not to
overclaim.

\begin{definition}[Bounded Ouroboros loop]
A loop is \emph{bounded} if it admits a well-founded measure: a ranking function
into a well-ordered set that strictly decreases on each governed step, with no
infinite descending chain.
\end{definition}

The in-tree scheduler-liveness formula (F2) realizes exactly this: a strictly
decreasing $\mathbb{N}$-valued measure that reaches $0$, proved sorry-free
(\S\ref{sec:f1f23}). Well-foundedness is the standard logical guarantee against
vicious self-reference; an unbounded Ouroboros would be precisely the epistemic
regress the substrate forecloses.

Crucially, the substrate does \textbf{not} claim to verify its own consistency.
G\"odel's second incompleteness theorem forbids a consistent system from proving its
own consistency, and Tarski forbids a language from defining its own truth
predicate. The substrate's response is \emph{stratification}: proofs are checked by
the Lean kernel; the kernel's run is checked by continuous integration; the CI is
gated by human sign-off. This is a hierarchy of meta-levels in the Tarskian sense,
not a single system bootstrapping its own truth (cf.\ \S\ref{sec:phil}, Objection~5).
The locked kernel (\S\ref{sec:kernel}), pinned at a fixed commit and held separate
from experimental work, \emph{is} that externalized meta-level.

\section{The Lutar invariant $\La$}
\label{sec:lambda}

\subsection{Definition and the trust-aggregation problem}
A governed action accumulates evidence along several axes (e.g.\ provenance,
policy-compliance, measurability-honesty, moral-grounding). Each axis yields a
sub-score in $[0,1]$. The governance question is how to combine $k$ such sub-scores
into a single trust verdict.

\begin{definition}[Lutar invariant]
For $x = (x_1,\dots,x_k) \in [0,1]^k$, the \emph{Lutar invariant} is the equal-weight
geometric mean
\[
  \La_k(x) \;=\; \Big(\textstyle\prod_{i=1}^{k} x_i\Big)^{1/k}.
\]
The equal exponents $1/k$ are the ``Egyptian unit-fraction weights''.
\end{definition}

The geometric mean is the natural aggregator for \emph{ratio-scale} evidence: it
commutes with a common rescaling of all axes (positive homogeneity), and a single
zero axis drives the verdict to zero (one disqualifying failure cannot be averaged
away). These are governance-desirable properties: a system that is excellent on
eight axes and catastrophic on one should not score well.

\subsection{The aggregation axioms A1--A5}
The substrate fixes five axioms on a candidate aggregator $\Phi:[0,1]^k\to[0,1]$.

\begin{axiom}[Monotonicity, A1]
$\Phi$ is non-decreasing in each argument.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Positive homogeneity, A2]
$\Phi(c\cdot x) = c\,\Phi(x)$ for $c>0$.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Idempotence / normalization, A3]
$\Phi(c,\dots,c) = c$.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Boundedness, A4]
$\Phi(x) \le \max_i x_i$.
\end{axiom}
\begin{axiom}[Permutation invariance / symmetry, A5]
$\Phi(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(k)}) = \Phi(x)$ for every permutation $\sigma$.
\end{axiom}

A5 was added as a structure field in a late round (PR \#148) precisely because the
literature (Kolmogorov 1930 \cite{kolmogorov1930}, Nagumo 1930, Acz\'el 1948
\cite{aczel1948}, Hardy--Littlewood--P\'olya 1934) confirms that A1--A4 \emph{alone}
do not force the geometric mean.

\subsection{The honest crux: unconditional uniqueness is FALSE}
\label{sec:false}
It is tempting to claim that $\La_k$ is the \emph{unique} $\Phi$ satisfying
A1--A5. \textbf{This is false, and we prove it false.}

\begin{theorem}[Refutation of unconditional uniqueness]
\label{thm:false}
\statusSorryFree\;\statusCI\;
There exists $\Phi \neq \La_k$ satisfying A1--A5. In particular, the max-aggregator
$\mathrm{maxAgg}(x) = \max_i x_i$ satisfies A1--A5 yet differs from $\La_k$:
at $(4,1)$ one has $\mathrm{maxAgg}=4$ while $\La_2(4,1)=2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean reference and proof]
The witness is \texttt{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda} (also realized as
\texttt{unconditional\_lambda\_is\_false}), machine-checked in-tree. The max
function is monotone (A1), positively homogeneous (A2), idempotent (A3), bounded by
itself hence $\le\max$ (A4), and symmetric (A5); evaluating at $(4,1)$ separates it
from the geometric mean by \texttt{decide}. \printax{maxAgg\_ne\_Lambda} reports
Lean-core axioms only.\footnote{Independent companions \texttt{aggMin} (min over
nine axes) and \texttt{aggMaxZ} exhibit the same failure on the
nine-axis bounty variant, also \texttt{decide}-checked.}
\end{proof}

This matches the classical theory exactly: Kolmogorov--Nagumo--de Finetti
characterize quasi-arithmetic means by symmetry, idempotency, monotonicity,
continuity, \emph{and} a \emph{bisymmetry / associativity / replacement} axiom
\cite{quasiarith,aczel1948}; the missing ingredient in A1--A5 is precisely that
last family. Acz\'el's two-variable result uses bisymmetry; the $n$-variable
refinement is due to Maksa--Mokken--M\"unnich \cite{naryqam2026}.

\subsection{The conditional theorem: uniqueness given factorization}
The maximal honestly-true uniqueness statement is conditional on factorization, and
it is fully proved in-tree.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness given factorization]
\label{thm:factors}
\statusSorryFree\;\statusCI\;
Let $\Phi$ satisfy A1--A5 and suppose $\Phi$ \emph{factors}, i.e.\ there exist
exponents $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k \ge 0$ with $\Phi(x) = \prod_i x_i^{\alpha_i}$
for all $x$. Then $\Phi = \La_k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean reference and proof sketch]
The Lean term is \texttt{lambda\_unique\_of\_factors} (Round-13). Given
factorization, idempotence (A3) forces $\sum_i \alpha_i = 1$ and symmetry (A5)
forces all $\alpha_i$ equal, whence each $\alpha_i = 1/k$ and $\Phi = \La_k$. The
exponent collapse uses \texttt{NNReal.rpow} arithmetic and a $\mathsf{Finset}$
induction. A companion lemma \texttt{lambda\_factors} (axiom-free, CI-green) shows
$\La_k$ \emph{itself} factors with exponents $1/k$, so the factorization hypothesis
is non-vacuous.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The conditional theorem: uniqueness under block-consistency}
The factorization hypothesis is itself opaque to a non-specialist. The substrate's
recommended route makes the extra assumption a \emph{governance-legible} axiom.

\begin{axiom}[Block-consistency / aggregation-invariance, A6$'$]
\label{ax:a6p}
Aggregating evidence within independent blocks and then across the block results
equals aggregating the flattened collection. Equivalently: the trust verdict does
not depend on how the auditor partitions the evidence into review blocks.
\end{axiom}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional uniqueness of $\La$ under A6$'$]
\label{thm:cond}
\statusAxiom\;\statusCI\;
Under $\{$A1, A2, A3, A4, A5$\}$ together with the single declared axiom
\texttt{A6'\_block\_consistent} (Axiom~\ref{ax:a6p}), the Lutar invariant $\La_k$ is
the unique normalized aggregator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Lean reference, axiom disclosure, and route]
The Lean term is \texttt{lambda\_unique\_under\_block}
(\texttt{Lutar/Wave4/LambdaBlockConsistency.lean}). It is \textbf{CI-green}: the
real \texttt{lutar-lean} CI \texttt{lake build} (a full kernel check) succeeds at
the recorded commit. The classical content is that aggregation-invariance forces the
quasi-arithmetic factorization that pins the geometric mean (Cs\'at\'o 2018
\cite{csato2018}, after Kolmogorov 1930 \cite{kolmogorov1930}); we formalize that
implication as the declared axiom and derive factorization $+$ uniqueness from it,
then discharge via Theorem~\ref{thm:factors}. \printax{lambda\_unique\_under\_block}
discloses exactly
$[\texttt{A6'\_block\_consistent},\,\texttt{propext},\,\texttt{Quot.sound},\,\texttt{Classical.choice}]$:
one declared, disclosed, non-core axiom and the trusted Lean core.
\end{proof}

A6$'$ is strictly \emph{weaker} and more governance-natural than the prior
bisymmetry axiom \texttt{A6\_bisymmetric}, which essentially asserts the
factorization directly; the team deliberately downgraded to A6$'$ to widen the gap
between premise and conclusion (\S\ref{sec:phil}). The cleanest \emph{published}
characterization is Acz\'el--Saaty 1983 \cite{aczelsaaty1983}: the geometric mean is
the unique quasi-arithmetic mean satisfying \emph{reciprocity} $+$ \emph{positive
homogeneity}, and positive homogeneity is already A2, so the only new commitment is
reciprocity.

\subsection{$\La$ is Conjecture~1 --- the precise claim structure}
\label{sec:conj}
We separate three propositions and assert the truth-value of each:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item[(U) Unconditional uniqueness] ``$\La$ is the unique $\Phi$ satisfying
    A1--A5.'' --- \textbf{FALSE} (Theorem~\ref{thm:false}).
  \item[(C) Conditional uniqueness] ``Given A1--A5 + A6$'$, $\La$ is unique.''
    --- \textbf{PROVEN, conditional} (Theorem~\ref{thm:cond}, CI-green, axiom disclosed).
  \item[(Conj) The governance conjecture] ``The \emph{right} axiomatization of a
    governed trust aggregator forces $\La$.'' --- \textbf{Conjecture~1}: a research
    hypothesis that A6$'$-type principles are the correct demand, \emph{not} a theorem.
\end{description}

\begin{conjecture}[The Lutar invariant; never a theorem]
\label{conj:lambda}
\statusConj\;
The equal-weight geometric mean $\La_k$ is the correct unique trust aggregator for
governed AI, in the sense that the governance-correct axiom (A6$'$-type
block-consistency / aggregation-invariance) is the right normative demand on a
governed aggregator. This is an open philosophical claim, not a mathematical
theorem; it is \emph{never} asserted as proven, and the unconditional form (U) is
\emph{provably false}.
\end{conjecture}

The honesty doctrine is simply: say (U) is false, (C) is proven-conditional, and
(Conj) is open --- and never let any reader collapse them. \S\ref{sec:phil} defends
why this calibration is epistemically virtuous rather than a weakness.

\section{The proof-trail / receipt architecture}
\label{sec:receipts}

Every governed action emits a \emph{receipt}: a canonical record of the action, its
inputs, the gate decision, and the $\La$ sub-scores. Receipts are hash-chained
(each entry commits to its predecessor) and Merkle-bound (a set of receipts commits
to a single root), so that tampering is detectable and inclusion is checkable. The
substrate formalizes the structural and security properties of this architecture in
Lean~4, with cryptographic hardness explicitly declared as an idealization rather
than proved.

\subsection{Structural correctness (sorry-free)}
The replay engine (F1) is deterministic: equal logs replay to equal final state, and
the folded state equals the last entry of the explicit replay trace (proved by
induction over the log; \printax{} reports \texttt{propext} only). Hash-chain
verification (F13) is sound: a verified chain implies all links match
(\printax{}: \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}). The Merkle inclusion checker
(F15) is correct-by-construction over an abstract hash.

\subsection{Security under declared idealizations (axiom-gated)}
Security properties --- tamper-evidence, attribution, inclusion-binding --- require
collision-resistance / unforgeability assumptions. We declare these as named axioms
and disclose them, following the ProVerif methodology of abstracting a primitive as
an axiom, proving the protocol against it, and discharging the axiom separately for a
concrete implementation \cite{proverif}. The declared axioms are
\texttt{hash\_collision\_resistant} (tamper-evidence, F13$'$),
\texttt{ecdsa\_unforgeable} (DSSE attribution, F14), and
\texttt{h2\_collision\_resistant} (inclusion-binding, F15). The Wave-3 Merkle work
adds \texttt{compression\_collision\_resistant} (Merkle--Damg{\aa}rd),

\section{Philosophical foundations: why block-consistency is the right axiom}
\label{sec:phil}

\subsection{The acceptability test for a governance axiom}
A primitive is acceptable to a regulator/auditor only if it is (i) \emph{interpretable}
as a governance norm a layperson-with-counsel would endorse, (ii) \emph{non-question-begging}
--- not secretly ``the answer is the geometric mean'' in disguise, and (iii)
\emph{independently motivated} --- defensible before $\La$ is mentioned. Block-consistency
(A6$'$) passes all three.

\paragraph{Interpretable.} Aggregation-invariance says the trust verdict must not
depend on how the auditor partitions the evidence into review blocks --- i.e.\
\emph{path-independence of the audit}. An auditor who could change a firm's score by
re-drawing departmental boundaries would, by that very fact, be running a manipulable
process. This is a fairness/objectivity requirement a regulator already believes in.

\paragraph{Non-question-begging.} Bisymmetry and associativity are formally near-equivalent
to the answer and can look, to a hostile technical advisor, like assuming the
conclusion. Aggregation-invariance is phrased entirely in terms of the audit process
(partition, aggregate, compare), with no reference to means, products, or exponents.
Cs\'at\'o (2018) \emph{derives} the row geometric mean from anonymity $+$
responsiveness $+$ aggregation-invariance \cite{csato2018} --- the geometric mean
falls out as a theorem rather than being smuggled in.

\paragraph{Independently motivated.} A6$'$ is a pre-existing, published axiom with a
stand-alone governance meaning, motivated before $\La$ enters. It is the same move
that underwrites objectivity in measurement theory (invariance under admissible scale
transformations \cite{foundmeas}) and in physics (covariance under coordinate choice):
the procedure must track the object, not the bookkeeping.

\subsection{When is a conditional uniqueness result legitimate, not overreach?}
A conditional ``if A6$'$ then $\La$-unique'' is trivially true as a material
implication if A6$'$ is strong enough, so a skeptical reviewer rightly worries about
antecedent-smuggling. Four well-established conditions distinguish a legitimate
conditional from a vacuous one, and the substrate meets all four:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Independently-motivated antecedent.} A6$'$ is published (Cs\'at\'o 2018)
    with a stand-alone meaning; we deliberately chose the \emph{weakest sufficient,
    most-independent} antecedent, downgrading from \texttt{A6\_bisymmetric}.
  \item \textbf{Non-vacuous instantiation.} \texttt{lambda\_factors} (axiom-free,
    CI-green) shows the factorization A6$'$ demands is realized by $\La$ itself.
  \item \textbf{Boundary established.} We proved $\lnot$(U) by explicit counterexample
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:false}); the conditional is the maximal true statement.
  \item \textbf{Disclosed at point of use.} Every theorem carries \printax{} exposing
    the A6$'$ dependency at compile time --- the Lakatosian virtue of explicit,
    revisable lemma-conditions \cite{lakatos}, the opposite of silent monster-barring.
\end{enumerate}
The residual gap --- \emph{is A6$'$ the philosophically correct demand?} --- is
genuinely open, which is exactly why $\La$ remains Conjecture~1. Calling it a theorem
would be the overreach; calling it a conjecture is the honest description of the open
\emph{philosophical} question, while the conditional theorem is the honest
description of the closed \emph{mathematical} one.

\subsection{``Conjecture~1'' as a calibration signal}
A reviewer who sees ``Conjecture~1'' beside ``machine-checked conditional theorem''
should read it as calibration in the sense of formal epistemology: the authors'
assertions track the evidence. A vendor who calls every plausible claim ``proven''
makes the label uninformative; the substrate's distinct, audited tiers (``locked''
$=$ 5 formulas; ``proven under declared axiom''; ``conjecture'') make its ``proven''
actually mean proven. Honesty here is not modesty; it is what makes any of the
positive claims credible \cite{reliabilism}.

\subsection{Doctrine-to-philosophy grounding map}
Each core substrate claim instantiates an established norm (it is \emph{legitimized},

\subsection{Steelman objections and honest rebuttals}
\paragraph{Objection 1 (strongest): ``A6$'$ is the geometric mean in a costume.''}
\emph{Rebuttal.} A6$'$ is independently published, phrased with no reference to means
or exponents (audit path-independence); we adopted the weakest sufficient, most
independent version, \emph{proved the unconditional claim false} so the conditional
is maximal, and disclose the axiom in every \printax{}. The honest residue is a
\emph{defensible normative design choice} --- ``a governed verdict must not depend on
how an auditor partitions the evidence'' --- from which the geometric mean is derived,
not assumed.

\paragraph{Objection 2: ``Motte-and-bailey.''}
\emph{Rebuttal.} The motte (conditional + Conjecture~1) and bailey (``$\La$ uniqueness'')
are labeled and separated \emph{in the artifacts themselves}: the lineage carries a
literal ``$\La$ --- Conjecture~1 (NOT a theorem)'' badge; proof reports lead with
``unconditional uniqueness STAYS FALSE.'' The fallacy requires equivocation; here the
three propositions (U/C/Conj) are explicitly distinguished at every assertion. We
concede the \emph{risk} that downstream summarization could collapse them --- which is
why the honesty doctrine is stated as ``must survive any summarization.''

\paragraph{Objection 3: ``Idealized crypto/A6$'$ axioms make the guarantees illusory.''}
\emph{Rebuttal.} Conceded and already disclosed: these are declared axioms, stated as
idealizations, not hardness proofs. Every applied formal result is conditional on its
abstraction boundary; the integrity move (ProVerif methodology \cite{proverif}) is to
make that boundary explicit. The guarantee is ``reliable given the standard
cryptographic assumptions the digital economy already relies on'' --- the same
conditional under which TLS and certificate transparency operate.

\paragraph{Objection 4: ``Arrow: aggregation is impossible.''}
\emph{Rebuttal.} Arrow/Sen concern aggregating \emph{ordinal preferences of distinct
agents} into a social ordering; $\La$ aggregates \emph{cardinal, ratio-scale evidence
sub-scores about one object} --- a measurement problem, not preference aggregation,
and not subject to Arrow's IIA-driven impossibility. The honest concession: where
trust dimensions encode incommensurable values, a scalar can mask a trade-off, so the
substrate retains the full sub-score vector and never lets $\La$ replace it.

\paragraph{Objection 5: ``G\"odel/Tarski: self-auditing is incoherent.''}
\emph{Rebuttal.} Conceded as a limit; it does not bite here. The substrate does
\emph{not} claim self-verification of its own consistency. It claims bounded
recursion with a well-founded measure (termination) and verification by an
\emph{external, stratified} stack (Lean kernel $\to$ CI $\to$ human sign-off). The
bottom turtle --- the Lean kernel, cryptographic, and human-trust assumptions --- is
finite and disclosed.

\paragraph{Objection 6: ``Trust can't be engineered.''}
\emph{Rebuttal.} Largely conceded, reframed as a feature. The philosophy of trust
distinguishes the \emph{attitude} of trust from the \emph{property} of
trustworthiness; $\La$ does not manufacture the attitude, it produces audited
evidence of trustworthiness-relevant properties on which a human's trust can
rationally rest. We claim only to make trustworthiness \emph{checkable}.
